## 1. FOUR OF THE SEVENTEEN SAMPLES ARE UNSTORABLE, AND THEY CARRIED THE SPREAD CLAIM

`data/samples.db` samples **1, 11, 14, 15** have the world dot at u≈0.96-0.97, v≈0.05-0.07, the
extreme corner of the world frame. That is **36-37° off axis, against a display half-angle of
24.1°**. The display physically cannot show anything there.

**Why nobody saw it:** `geometry.geometric_pixel` CLIPS to [0,1]. An off-screen target silently
became an edge pixel, the operator nudged the overlay onto whatever they could actually see, and
the sample stored looking entirely ordinary. Sample 1 even scored the **best error in the set,
0.9 px**, because the human had pushed the dot into the same corner the clip produced. *A check
agreeing with a clamp is not a check.* This is the ninth-and-tenth instance of a pattern I have
been logging all along: a number went green for a reason unrelated to what it claimed to measure.

| | all 17 | the clean 13 |
|---|---|---|
| world-dot u span | **0.467** | **0.056** |
| approved display-u span | 0.617 | 0.126 |
| geometry error, median | 26.9 px | 25.9 px |
| geometry error, p95 | **693 px** | 60 px |

**The recorded claim "world dot span 0.37-0.48 … the feature spread is finally healthy" was
produced entirely by the four poisoned samples.** Without them the run covered **5.6% of the
world field**, barely better than the 0.026-0.052 of the runs it was being contrasted against as
"degenerate". Three of the four also carry the only gross errors in the set (640, 690, 706 px).

**FIXED, and it cannot recur:**
- `geometry.offscreen(features)` / `geometry.offaxis_deg(features)`, with
  `geometric_pixel_raw` exposing the unclipped pixel. `geometric_pixel` still clips, because a
 render loop cannot draw off-canvas, the bug was never the clip, it was that nothing else could
  see it had happened.
- `main.py` **refuses the approve** and says why, and draws it **on the display** , 
 `TARGET IS 37 DEG OFF AXIS / OUTSIDE THE 48 DEG DISPLAY / turn towards it`, because the
  operator is wearing the rig and the terminal does not reach them.
- The `geometry.py` selftest pins the **four literal stored feature vectors** as must-reject and
  four real good ones plus a legitimate display-edge target as must-keep. A guard that throws away
  real data would be worse than the problem.
- `reseat.py --check` reports contamination FIRST, before any statistic computed over the samples.

## 2. `DISPLAY_FOV_DEG = 48.25` IS NOT PINNED TO ±0.1: IT IS PINNED TO ABOUT ±3°

Bootstrapping the fit that produced it (400 resamples):

| set | best fit | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| clean 13 | 49.00° | **47.2 – 52.8** |
| all 17 | 49.00° | 46.7 – 52.8 |

The profile is not a parabola, it is bumpy noise: 25.9 px at 47.0°, 26.5 at 48.0, 20.6 at 49.0,
25.6 at 50.0. **Thirteen samples spanning 5.6% of the field cannot pin an angular scale**, there
is almost no lever arm.

**What stays true:** 48.25 beats the old assumed 50.0 *in sample*, and the "keep the fitted value,
do not substitute the physical one" reasoning in `geometry.py` is unchanged and still right.
**What changes:** the *precision* implied by quoting "48.25" was never there, and since 2° of FOV
error costs 18 px, the constant carries roughly as much uncertainty as the residual it was
credited with removing. **Removing the 4 poisoned samples does not move it** (48.20 → 49.00, both
deep inside the CI), so the contamination did not bias this constant, it just never had the
spread to determine it. **Pinning it needs a run that spans the field**, which is now the second
requirement on the next calibration.

## 3. THE EYE-CORNER CORRECTION IS A NEAR-FIELD TERM. IT IS PARALLAX, AND IT DIES AS 1/DISTANCE

Measured off `geometry.py`, overlay shift for a **3 mm** change in how the glasses sit:

| target distance | 0.4 m | 0.7 m | 1 m | 2 m | 5 m | **20 m** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| overlay shift | 14.3 px | 8.6 px | 6.1 px | 3.1 px | 1.3 px | **0.3 px** |

**This is strategically important and was not previously written down.** The end goal is overlaying
people and cars that are FAR AWAY. Out there the eye-corner correction is worth **a third of a
pixel**, it cannot be an accuracy lever for the actual product goal. Its value is near-field, plus
what it already does: gating whether a frame is usable, and detecting slippage on a zip-tied mount.
This is the same shape as the existing far-field note (the ~34 mm camera-to-eye offset subtends
0.096° at 20 m), and for the same reason: both are parallax.

## 4. THE 2026-08-03 GAIN SWEEP WAS UNDERPOWERED, NOT A NULL RESULT: AND NOW IT SAYS SO

`reseat.py --check` reproduces the old sweep exactly (26.9 px at gain 0.00, rising monotonically , 
the recorded numbers) and then shows why it decided nothing:

```
seat span            0.00 mm across 1 seating
effect of gain 1.0   median 0.85 px, max 1.91 px
regression gain      23.22 +- 61.77
VERDICT              CANNOT DECIDE
```

The term could move the overlay by **less than one pixel** on that data. Writing "the eye-corner
correction does not help" would have been a claim the data cannot support, and `EYE_SHIFT_GAIN`
would have stayed 0.0 for a reason that was never tested.

**`reseat.py` can now return three answers, and the third is the one the old sweep could not
express: SET IT, LEAVE IT OFF (a real null), or CANNOT DECIDE.**

## 5. THE MEDIAN-ERROR GRID IS THE WRONG ESTIMATOR. USE THE REGRESSION

Sweeping gains and taking the lowest median error is far too blunt for a term worth a few px under
a ~27 px systematic. Instead, fit the gain directly:

```
Y - pixel(gain=0)  =  A . [1, world_u, world_v]  +  g . (pixel(gain=1) - pixel(gain=0))
```

The affine term absorbs every registration error that is a smooth function of target direction , 
display scale, distortion, kappa, world-camera error, i.e. exactly the ~27 px this project has
been unable to see past. `g` is then identified only by samples pointing the same way whose eye
features differ, which is what re-seating creates and a single seating does not contain.

Measured over 10 seeds per design, recovering a **planted gain of 1.0**:

| protocol | grid rms error | **regression rms error** |
|---|---|---|
| recommended (6 mm re-seat, 0.4-1 m, n=32) | 0.34 (mean 0.90) | **0.12 (mean 0.99)** |
| marginal (3 mm re-seat, 1-3 m, n=32) | 0.56, **mean 0.53** | 0.54, mean 0.91 |

The grid does not merely scatter, **it reads LOW**, towards zero, because a small term cannot move
the median of a widely-scattered distribution. That is the shape of the 2026-08-03 result.

## 6. THE PROTOCOL FOR THE NEXT RUN, DERIVED RATHER THAN GUESSED: `reseat.py --power`

| protocol | n | effect | recovered gain | SE | detects a true gain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-08-03 (no re-seat, 1-3 m) | 17 | 0.2 px | 1.17 | 3.50 | **0%** |
| off-and-on re-seat, 1-3 m | 32 | 1.8 px | 0.86 | 0.38 | **50%** |
| off-and-on re-seat, 1-3 m | 64 | 2.1 px | 0.97 | 0.20 | 100% |
| **re-seat BIG (6 mm), 1-3 m** | 32 | 3.6 px | 0.90 | 0.19 | 83% |
| **re-seat BIG, CLOSE 0.4-1 m** | **32** | **9.3 px** | **0.94** | **0.09** | **100%** |

**The handoff's plan, "take them off and put them back on between batches", would have been a
coin flip** (50% at 32 samples). Two free changes make it certain at the same sample count:

1. **RE-SEAT DELIBERATELY DIFFERENTLY, not just off-and-on.** Move the glasses up/down/left/right
   on the nose by several mm between batches. The mount is zip-tied, so this is easy and it is
 also realistic, it is the slippage the correction exists for.
2. **KEEP THE TARGET CLOSE, 0.4-1.0 m, for this measurement.** The term is parallax; at 2-3 m it
   is worth ~3 px and is invisible. (This is only for measuring the gain. The gain is a
   dimensionless constant, so it transfers to far targets even though its *effect* there is small.)
3. **COVER THE SAME PART OF THE FIELD AT EVERY SEATING.** If each seating looks at only one
   target, the seat is perfectly aliased with target direction and no estimator can separate them
, measured: independence drops to 1%, gain scatter to ±1.2. `reseat.py` detects this and
   refuses to answer rather than reporting a confident wrong number.
4. **SPAN THE FIELD** (item 2 above): the last run covered 5.6% of it.

## >>> THE BIG CORRECTION: LAST SESSION RAN IN **LOCKED** MODE, NOT FOLLOW <<<

My notes from 2026-08-03 said I had follow mode on and that this was correct. **I had LOCKED
on.** I never verified it at the time; I wrote it down as settled and then reasoned from it for
a day.

**This reaches further than the note:**
- **The 17 real calibration samples were captured in ANCHOR/3DoF**, the mode my own argument says
  makes the map ill-posed, because the optics displace the image by a head-pose-dependent amount
  before it reaches the eye. They are not a clean (features -> pixel) set.
- **`DISPLAY_FOV_DEG = 48.25` was fitted to those samples**, so it absorbed the glasses' internal
  stabiliser too. A candidate explanation for the unreconciled gap between the wall measurement
 (46.71 deg) and the data fit (48.25), previously blamed on distortion and eye position alone.
- **It explains today's regression with no software cause.** On the first run in FOLLOW mode the
  overlay followed my head movements instead of countering them. That is what follow mode plus a
  17.9 fps / 62 ms loop does. Last session the glasses' stabiliser did the counter-rotation in
  hardware at near-zero latency. **Tracking felt better in locked mode because the hardware was
  doing the work, not because anything was better calibrated.**

## WHAT WAS MEASURED WHEN THE RUN CAME BACK EMPTY (0 samples, no ENTER pressed)

Two hypotheses raised and **both killed by measurement**, recorded because the wrong fix was
nearly applied twice:

1. **"My off-screen banner is hiding the dot."** `Overlay.render` does suppress the red dot while a
   banner shows, which would look exactly like the reported fault. **Measured: `offscreen` fired on
   0% of 89 live frames.** Not the cause.
2. **"Deleting the 4 outliers let the learned residual survive cross-validation."** Plausible, a
   residual fitted to 13 samples spanning 5.6% of the field would extrapolate violently.
   **Measured: `W is None`, `_residual_helps = False`, pure geometry.** Not the cause.

A third scare was self-inflicted: `cal.predict` measured **438 px** of scatter against
`geometry.geometric_pixel`'s 4 px, from two runs taken minutes apart. Re-measured **on the same
frames in the same loop**, they are **bit-identical (max diff 0.000000)**, both at 7 px. The 438 px
was real head motion. *Never compare two live measurements taken at different times.*

**Everything measurable is healthy and matches last session:**

| | 2026-08-04 | 2026-08-03 |
|---|---|---|
| dot detector | sd 0.004, 40/40, 0 edge picks | "stable when well framed" |
| eye lock | eyeL **93%** (99% in band), eyeR **91%** (100%) | ~90% |
| saturation worn | 4.1% / 1.0% | 0.7% / 0.7% |
| geometry vs `cal.predict` | bit-identical, sd 7 px | pure geometry |
| IMU | ON, R² 0.99/0.85, velocity sd 0.025 frame/s at rest | same |

**So there was no software regression. The difference was the display mode**, which no measurement
of ours could have found because it happens inside the glasses.
